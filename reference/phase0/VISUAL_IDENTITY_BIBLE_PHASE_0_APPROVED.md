# HERR ARCHIVES — WORLD WAR ONE
## Visual Identity Bible · Phase 0

**Status:** Review draft for approval  
**Scope:** Visual identity and review boards only. No production scenes, Remotion implementation, animation assets, or full storyboard work is authorised in this phase.

---

## 1. Locked creative direction

| Decision | Selected direction |
|---|---|
| Illustration direction | **Cinematic hybrid** — detailed, hand-painted European environments combined with animated 2D storybook characters |
| Character direction | **Storybook** — approachable, expressive and readable, while historically grounded |
| Map direction | **Atlas** — vintage cartographic materials with clean, precise routes and labels |
| Colour | Rich, warm, saturated European storybook palette; not neon and not muddy sepia |
| Environments | Close, detailed, inhabited and layered; avoid empty backdrops |
| Transitions | Textured paint wipes, reveals and dissolves |
| Map routing / pointing | Clean vector paths, markers, arrows and labels; **no paint-brush routing effects** |
| Archive | Selective, purposeful use of supplied photographs; never insert images merely because they exist |

### One-sentence identity

**Herr Archives tells serious history inside a vivid, hand-painted European storybook world, using expressive characters, cinematic environments and precise atlas graphics.**

The storybook character treatment should make the history accessible, not childish. The environments can be lush and colourful; the historical subject, acting and composition must retain weight and credibility.

---

## 2. Visual principles

1. **World before backdrop.** Every location should feel inhabited and geographically specific. Compose foreground, midground, background and atmosphere as a coherent environment.
2. **Characters perform.** A character is not a portrait placed in a scene. It must be able to look, listen, gesture, move, react and interact.
3. **Clarity before decoration.** Dates, routes, names and statistics must be immediately readable. Decorative texture must never obscure information.
4. **Paint belongs to the world.** Use watercolor, gouache, ink and paper texture in the illustration and for scene transitions. Do not use brush splashes to draw routes, point at locations or annotate data.
5. **Archive is evidence.** Use a real photograph when it adds provenance, emotional impact or a useful historical contrast. Otherwise, use an original illustrated reconstruction.
6. **Consistency across variety.** Scenes can vary in composition and palette emphasis, but line character, material, typography, character proportions and graphic rules remain recognisable.

## 3. Colour system

The following palette is the working master palette. Hex values are the written source of truth; generated board lettering and sampled image colours are only visual references.

| Token | Hex | Primary use |
|---|---|---|
| Sky Blue | `#63B5D6` | Daylight skies, distant atmosphere, water highlights |
| River Teal | `#319C9A` | Rivers, cool environmental accents, select map fills |
| Terracotta | `#C96B50` | Roofs, brick, warm architecture and emphasis |
| Foliage | `#6F9959` | Trees, parks, countryside and natural forms |
| Warm Plaster | `#F0D8AD` | Sunlit facades, warm stone and interiors |
| Marigold | `#E6B74F` | Sunlight, brass, selected focal accents |
| Slate Blue | `#537C9B` | Uniforms, shaded architecture and secondary cool forms |
| Ink Navy | `#293746` | Primary outlines, deep shadows, key text |
| Burgundy Accent | `#8B3A3A` | Chapter ribbons, warning / emphasis accents, seals |
| Paper Cream | `#EFE6D2` | Paper fields, cards and neutral backdrops |

### Colour behaviour

- Use sky blue, teal, foliage and warm plaster to make the world feel open and lively.
- Terracotta and marigold add warmth; use them to create focal paths through a composition.
- Ink navy replaces pure black for most outlines and shadow accents.
- Burgundy is a restrained editorial accent, not a full-screen default.
- Do not apply the full palette at equal intensity to every frame. Establish a dominant local palette per location, then use accents to guide attention.
- For night, crisis and wartime sequences, reduce saturation and shift toward slate, ink navy and controlled warm light without abandoning the project's line and texture language.

**Suggested balance, adjustable by scene:** 45% local environment colours, 25% warm stone/paper neutrals, 20% cool secondary colours, 10% focal accents. This is a composition guide, not a mandatory pixel ratio.

## 4. Environment art direction

### Overall treatment

Detailed painted environments with a hand-drawn ink contour. Combine the depth and atmosphere of a historical painting with the staging and readability of a 2D animated world. The background must feel close enough to inhabit, not like a distant matte painting behind isolated characters.

### Required depth planes

1. **Foreground:** branches, awnings, signboards, balconies, railings, street furniture, passing pedestrians or other occluders.
2. **Midground:** the primary action, characters, vehicles and key architecture.
3. **Background:** facades, rooflines, bridges, hills, distant buildings and skyline landmarks.
4. **Atmosphere:** sky, clouds, haze, sunlight, rain, smoke, steam or dust.
5. **Ambient life:** subtle motion independent from the main action (flags, leaves, water, crowd loops, drifting smoke, wheels or tram movement where appropriate).

### Composition rules

- Prefer close and medium-wide compositions with foreground framing over empty establishing shots.
- Let architecture overlap and frame characters; use balconies, arches, trees, windows and street props to build depth.
- Use parallax deliberately. Foreground movement should be stronger than background movement, but avoid constant camera drift.
- Keep a clear stage for the narrated action. Background detail must not compete with faces, gestures or important objects.
- Use architecture appropriate to the specific location; Sarajevo should not look like generic Paris, and Vienna should not look like a generic central-European street.

### Location language

- **Sarajevo:** Miljacka river promenade, bridges, mixed Austro-Hungarian / Ottoman-influenced urban fabric, hillside setting, period street furniture and vehicles. Do not invent precise architecture where source references do not support it.
- **Vienna:** grand imperial facades, formal squares, monumental civic architecture and ordered streets.
- **Berlin:** monumental government architecture, broad avenues, imperial symbols and industrial / rail infrastructure.
- **London:** civic stone architecture, riverfronts, formal streets and restrained urban atmosphere.
- **Paris / 1900 Fair:** exhibition architecture, glass-and-iron structures, crowds and electric-era optimism.
- **European countryside:** rail bridges, hills, fields, telegraph poles, stations and weather systems.

These are visual direction notes, not a substitute for location reference research during scene production.

## 5. Character design system

### Character direction

Storybook characters with historically grounded costumes and distinguishing features. Faces and poses should be clear at 1080p and still recognisable at reduced preview resolution. Proportions may be slightly softened or enlarged for expressiveness, but must not become chibi, anime, rubber-hose or slapstick caricature.

### Proportion and silhouette

- Slightly enlarged head and hands for readable expression and gesture; keep adult proportions and period clothing credible.
- Use distinctive silhouette first: hairstyle, facial hair, headwear, uniform cut, collar, medals and posture.
- Maintain the same head-to-body ratio, shoulder width, costume construction and facial landmarks across views.
- Avoid making a historical figure recognisable only through a caption.

### Required model-sheet coverage

For each recurring Tier A character, create:

- Front, three-quarter, profile and back views.
- Neutral, speaking, concerned, angry, surprised and determined expressions as appropriate.
- Idle, walk, turn, gesture, reach, react and sit / stand poses as needed by the script.
- Costume reference for the relevant year and context.
- A small set of hand poses and facial / eye directions.

### Character tiers

| Tier | Use | Design requirement |
|---|---|---|
| A — Hero | Recurring figures central to the story: Franz Ferdinand, Sophie, Wilhelm II, Nicholas II, Gavrilo Princip and other major dramatised figures | Full model sheets and reusable rigs |
| B — Supporting | Key officials, diplomats, officers, drivers and secondary named figures | Simplified but consistent rigs or modular pose sets |
| C — Crowd | Police, soldiers, civilians and background groups | Modular silhouettes, varied costume palettes and limited loops |

### Acting rules

- Every important action has anticipation, action, follow-through and settle / reaction.
- Gestures should have a clear target and reason. No idle waving or arbitrary limb motion.
- Eyes and head lead many movements; torso and limbs follow with appropriate delay.
- Weight must transfer during walking, stopping, turning and reaching.
- Reactions should be staged and readable; do not animate all characters identically or simultaneously.
- Use hand-drawn key poses for important turns and emotional beats. Rig interpolation supports the drawings; it does not replace them.

**Historical likeness note:** final character sheets must be checked against reliable portraits, photographs and period costume references. AI-generated review images are style explorations, not authoritative likeness references.

## 6. Atlas map and information graphics

### Design language

Vintage atlas materials, restrained paper texture and historically appropriate country colours, combined with clean, modern information hierarchy. Map accuracy is non-negotiable: an attractive but incorrect border is a failed map.

### Graphic primitives

- Country fills: muted, distinct colour families with adequate contrast.
- Borders: fine, stable vector lines; use different line weights for international boundaries, regional boundaries and coastlines.
- Routes: clean SVG paths with solid / dashed styles as context requires.
- Direction: small, restrained arrowheads following route geometry.
- Locations: simple circles, rings or small pin markers; one consistent visual system.
- Labels: legible sans-serif, controlled hierarchy, no hand lettering for dense geographic labels.
- Callouts: paper / parchment cards with consistent corner, border and shadow treatment.
- Animated reveal: animate path length or marker position, not brush splashes.

### Explicit prohibition

**Never use watercolor brush strokes, paint splashes or paint wipes to indicate routes, troop movement, pointing, geographic boundaries or map annotations.** Paint effects are reserved for transitions between scenes and for the organic illustration itself.

### Data and map integrity

- Use locally stored, versioned GeoJSON / TopoJSON or other reviewed source data in later phases.
- Every historical boundary must carry a date and source note in the production manifest.
- Do not assume present-day borders represent 1914 borders.
- Do not rely on live map tiles or live geocoding during final rendering.
- Generated concept maps are not valid geographic source data; they only demonstrate material, colour and graphic style.

### Later technical direction (not implemented in Phase 0)

The current candidate stack for Phase 2 is React Simple Maps with `d3-geo` and `topojson-client`, drawing local historical boundary data into SVG. Package choice remains subject to a small technical spike and licence / compatibility review.

## 7. Texture, line and paint language

### Core materials

- Watercolor / gouache washes with controlled pigment pooling.
- Fine paper grain and subtle edge wear.
- Dark navy or warm charcoal ink contours; avoid a uniform pure-black outline.
- Selective pencil / hatch detail in architecture, clothing and props.
- Painted clouds, trees, stone, water and smoke with visible brush character.

### Texture hierarchy

1. Primary subject outlines and face details must remain crisp.
2. Midground and environmental outlines may be looser and more irregular.
3. Background texture should be softer and lower contrast.
4. Paper grain must be subtle enough not to degrade small text or map labels.
5. Watercolor variation must be deterministic in rendered output; no uncontrolled random flicker.

### Frame boil

Use controlled drawing variation for painted outlines and selected organic fills. The reference's 12-drawing-per-second boil can be a starting reference while the delivered composition remains 24 fps. Do not boil typography, map labels, UI, route geometry or critical facial landmarks. Keep per-element seeds / stable variation so one element's texture does not change because another element was added.

## 8. Transition and camera language

### Paint effects — allowed uses

- Painted reveal into a new location.
- Dry-brush wipe between scenes or chapters.
- Ink / watercolor dissolve for a time jump or narrative shift.
- Paper / archival texture blend when moving between an original photograph and an illustrated reconstruction.

### Paint effects — prohibited uses

- Map routes, arrows or location pointers.
- Underlining names, statistics or important text where a clean line is clearer.
- Constant decorative splashes throughout a scene.
- Covering an important historical face, object or piece of evidence without narrative purpose.

### Camera rules

- Camera movement must reveal, follow, emphasise or reframe an action.
- Use establishing wides sparingly; favour motivated push-ins, pans, tracking shots, reaction close-ups and match cuts.
- Do not use constant sinusoidal drift as a default animation layer.
- Use parallax to support depth, not as a substitute for character or object movement.
- Keep transitions motivated by shape, movement, direction, colour or story connection.

## 9. Typography and graphic identity

### Proposed type system

| Role | Typeface direction | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Main series / chapter titles | Cinzel or a similar classical display serif | World War One title, chapter openers and major section titles |
| Editorial headings | Playfair Display | Short editorial heads and emphasis cards |
| Narrative / document text | Source Serif 4 | Readable historical excerpts, letters and longer on-screen text |
| Labels / data / maps | Inter | Dates, map labels, markers, statistics and UI |

Use a restrained hierarchy: usually one display line, one supporting line and small utility labels. Avoid permanent bottom title plaques on every shot. Text should be integrated into the composition as a letter, newspaper, map label, date card or brief editorial overlay only when it adds information.

### Brand treatment

- “HERR ARCHIVES” as a compact wordmark in a classical serif or small-caps treatment.
- A restrained burgundy or terracotta rule / stamp as an accent.
- Avoid a mascot-based identity; the series identity should feel archival, editorial and illustrated.
- The logo lockup is a proposal; the exact master logo is not being redrawn or approved in Phase 0.

## 10. Archival photography policy

- Use an archival photograph only when it provides evidence, provenance, a recognisable real-world reference or a purposeful contrast with a reconstruction.
- Do not force the use of every supplied image.
- Preserve the image's original historical content. Do not generate missing details into a photograph or present a composite as a real archive image.
- Use captions only when identity, date and context are verified.
- Use painted frames, paper mounts, crop movement or ink transitions sparingly; do not turn every photograph into the same moving card.
- Keep the disputed / misidentified Gavrilo Princip arrest image excluded, as requested.

---

## 11. Motion quality bar

Every shot should identify:

- **Primary action:** the single event the viewer must understand.
- **Secondary action:** character reaction, environmental motion or prop follow-through.
- **Camera behaviour:** why the camera moves, where it starts and where it ends.
- **Timing:** anticipation, action, impact, follow-through and settle.
- **Visual hierarchy:** what the viewer should look at first, second and third.

A head bob, tiny photograph drift or subtle camera zoom does not count as meaningful animation by itself. Every important shot must contain a readable event or an intentionally held dramatic composition.

## 12. Do / don't checklist

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Build detailed, close, layered European worlds | Use empty generic gradients behind characters |
| Use warm, saturated but controlled colour | Push every scene into neon or muddy sepia |
| Design consistent storybook character rigs | Use one static portrait with bobbing |
| Use clean atlas routes and labels | Paint routes with splashes or brush strokes |
| Reserve paint wipes for scene transitions | Use a paint wipe for every information graphic |
| Use archive photos selectively and accurately | Use every photo or invent captions |
| Use purposeful camera choreography | Add constant camera drift to every shot |
| Keep map geometry sourced and dated | Treat generated maps as historically accurate |
| Review frame strips and full motion | Approve a scene from a single still |
